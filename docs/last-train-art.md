# Last Train visual assets

All artwork and Inter are self-hosted. There are no runtime image or font provider calls. Public appearance is independent of secret role, and survives recovery, promotion, seat shuffles and new games.

- `passengers.webp`: twelve public portraits, four columns × three rows, 1448 × 1086. The fictional cast is Chinese, Thai and Vietnamese, with varied ages and distinct clothing.
- `seated.webp`: matching public characters, each physically drawn on a velvet sofa seat. Person and cushion are one sprite, so responsive scaling cannot detach them. The atlas is four columns × three rows. The renderer uses measured row boundaries per column plus a narrow clip at the crown to exclude adjoining sprite pixels. The previous separate sofa layer is no longer used.
- `seated-back.webp`: the same twelve public characters and velvet seats from behind, in a four-column × three-row atlas. Near-side seats face the other travellers across the aisle, with small public portrait badges retaining recognition. Secret roles never choose seating artwork.
- `lounge-table.webp`: retained as unused earlier artwork; the lounge now has an open aisle without a table.
- `seated-warm.webp`, `seated-back-warm.webp`: revised lounge-only seated atlases with matching oxblood upholstery, warm amber light and contact shadows. They keep the same 4 × 3 cast mapping and dimensions as the existing seated atlases; cabin seating keeps its earlier artwork. The lounge has one shared camera, matching light temperature and softer floor shadows.
- `standing.webp`: matching twelve public characters standing head-to-toe, on transparency, four columns × three rows on a square 1254 × 1254 atlas. Each cell has a 3:4 aspect ratio. Used only by the final winning-team lineup.
- `patients.webp`: matching unconscious characters, without injuries or gore, on transparency. Each character is a limp, reclined, closed-eye figure with relaxed arms, rather than a curled sleeping pose. Art regions are 362 × 280 at x = column × 362, y = 108 / 436 / 752. These measured bands account for the generous vertical spacing in the source image; they are not square portrait cells.
- `cabin.webp`: hand-free first-person private cabin view. The compact scene uses this wide composition on phones as well, so a short image does not crop a tall portrait down to the sofa base. `cabin-mobile.webp` is retained as earlier artwork.
- `cabin-floor.webp`: empty cabin wall and carpet, behind the seated Saboteur team or the Medic’s patient. There is no second sofa underneath a character’s integrated seat.
- `lounge-room.webp`, `lounge-room-mobile.webp`: a continuous carpeted observation room with a matching camera and ground plane. Seats and captions share one responsive coordinate system inside the artwork. Rear seats are smaller than front seats. Adjacent seats are grouped into pairs with wider aisles between pairs; every row uses the same placement without a central table gap. Speaking order remains left to right, then onto the next row (six across on larger screens, four on phones). The older `lounge.webp` is retained for the finale.
- `platform.webp`: departure scene, lobby/introduction backdrop and elimination backdrop.
- `boarding.c0472ce51f03.mp4`, `boarding-poster.webp`: approved LTX boarding clip, delivered locally as a 6-second 1280 × 720 H.264 MP4 with its original AAC soundtrack (about 2.13 MB), and its first-frame WebP fallback. The versioned MP4 is browser-cacheable for repeat games. The video depicts an atmospheric group; the passenger checklist retains the actual room identities. See `last-train-boarding-video.md` for provenance and review limits.
- `train-icon.webp`: a centered frontal locomotive, used by the game selector and entrance illustration.
- `medicine-heal.svg`, `medicine-poison.svg`: illustrated glass medicine bottles with gold stoppers, green healing medicine and violet poison. Decorative icons have empty alternative text; each button has an explicit accessible name.
- `roles.webp`: private role illustrations and the public role catalog. Role indices remain Passenger 0, Careful 1, Brave 2, Witness 3, Speaker 4, Inspector 5, Medic 6, Trainee 7, Saboteur 8, Blocker 9. The final two cells are used for Steward (10) and Forger (11).

Public portrait indices, left to right and top to bottom, use these identifying outfits: jade blouse / navy suit / burgundy blouse / camel blazer and glasses; blue coat / silver beard and wine jacket / ivory shirt and navy cardigan / violet blouse; emerald coat / magenta blouse / teal jacket / silver bob and red coat. The same index chooses portraits, seated bodies and patient bodies. Living players’ secret roles never choose their public appearance. Revealed out players remain as faded figures, with a red cross on their seat number and a green GOOD or red BAD label.

The Medic’s patient layer is rendered only during the living, unblocked Medic’s turn, using `me.patientId` already provided by the private API view. Other phones receive no patient identity. A heal changes the label to HEAL GIVEN; it does not promise survival, since protection plus healing can still cause overdose.

Cabin artwork and action controls are separate siblings: choices sit below the image. The power limit and effect/warning are separate paragraphs, with compact Choose and Skip buttons. The patient scene frames the carpet more closely, and the Saboteur seating is scaled to the shorter cabin view. Lobby panels, discussion, chat, dialogs and the journey log share walnut, wine and champagne colors. The private role drawer is hosted in a dedicated fixed layer sized to the visual viewport. It opens by tap, drag, touch swipe or keyboard. Closed content is invisible, inert and excluded from the accessibility tree. It collapses on phase/active-role changes, remains stable on polls and does not inherit scene animations. Inter is distributed under the included SIL Open Font License in `public/fonts/Inter-OFL.txt`.

Vote results have an original train-themed cinematic: a passenger ticket enters and is stamped CANCELLED, then reveals the removed player’s alignment while station lights pass. The former black punch-hole circle has been removed. It uses the public portrait, not the hidden role illustration. It lasts 4.8 seconds within the existing 12-second result phase, pauses with the room, can be skipped with View votes/Escape, does not replay on polling/reload, and is omitted when no one is removed. Reduced motion gives a static result. The illustrated vote record is three columns on larger screens and two on phones, with avatars, names, Skip and double-vote labels.

Use viewport screenshots when showing fixed overlays. Full-page screenshots include a viewport-fixed drawer at the capture viewport’s location inside the long image; they do not show where it would remain while a person scrolls.

The host’s Start Journey and Another Journey actions share a bright gold-foil ticket with notched sides, an engraved inner border, a perforated divider and a slow reflective glint. The glint is disabled for reduced motion and disabled buttons. A multicolor train crest combines teal bodywork, blue glass, burgundy trim and gold rails across the header, tickets, boarding and winner screen. Its accessible name remains START JOURNEY. The private notebook floats on the right in the lobby and every game phase. It is a separate persistent modal, outside the polled scene tree. Players can type or draw with touch, pen or mouse, erase and undo (including undoing Clear drawing). Vector points use normalized coordinates for resizing. Text and drawing autosave locally under a room/player/match key; lobby notes carry into the first journey, and a later new game gets a new notebook. Notes are never added to API payloads or shared chat and do not transfer to another device.

The notebook is now a nearly full-height writing desk, with six individually persisted ink colors and selection suppression limited to drawing/chrome. Its floating control sits at the top right so it does not cover faces in the lounge or winner lineup. Boarding uses the approved 6-second video with sequential boarding checks below. The full composition is contained in the scene on desktop and mobile, with a still poster for reduced motion or unavailable playback.

- `roles-additional.webp`: seven generated human role portraits matching the original purple-and-gold illustrated style, four columns × two rows. Top row: Conductor, Night Porter, Guard, Signalman. Bottom row: Framer, Silencer, Smuggler, unused. Stowaway is excluded. These replace the temporary symbol crests in private role cards and the public role catalog. Public player avatars remain independent of secret roles.
